# Masjid Valley — Full Game Outline

## 1. The Idea in One Line

A cozy Stardew-style farming game about restoring a rundown masjid and bringing its community back to life over one Hijri year. The masjid *is* the community center.

## 2. The Story

### Premise
You return to your grandmother's neighborhood after years away. The masjid where you grew up is falling apart — the prayer hall carpet is worn thin, the minaret needs repair, the wudu area leaks, the library shelves are empty, and the garden is a tangle of weeds. The community has drifted away. Your grandmother leaves you the keys and one request: **bring the masjid back to life.**

### The Year — Five Acts

**Act 1 — Arrival (Muharram – Safar)**
You arrive to a quiet, dusty masjid. You meet Imam Yusuf, who hands you the keys and the first job: clear the courtyard and fix the garden wall. You learn the basics — till, plant, water, harvest. The masjid cat appears on the second day and decides you belong here. The neighborhood is polite but distant. The masjid feels empty.

**Act 2 — The Community Wakes (Rabi al-Awwal – Jumada)**
People start coming back. You restore the wudu area and the library shelves. Umm Maryam reopens the children's classes. The shaykh's house opens to you — you can now ask him for advice, rulings, and duas. The hunting grounds open, and the shaykh teaches you zabiha before you hunt for food. Hassan opens the community kitchen and teaches you the cooking minigame. The first communal meal happens in the courtyard.

**Act 3 — Preparing for Ramadan (Rajab – Sha'ban)**
The big push. You restore the prayer hall carpet and repair the minaret. Trust deepens with the neighbors and their personal quest lines resolve — the two neighbors arguing over the garden make peace with the shaykh's help, the market trader offers you a shady deal and you turn it down. The whole neighborhood starts planning the Ramadan iftars together.

**Act 4 — Ramadan (Ramadan)**
The masjid is full every night. You cook iftar in the kitchen each evening, the community gathers, Taraweeh fills the prayer hall, and Laylat al-Qadr is a special quiet night event. The final big restoration — the dome — completes during the last ten nights. The masjid glows.

**Act 5 — Eid and After (Shawwal – Dhul-Hijjah)**
Eid al-Fitr: celebration in the courtyard, everyone you helped is there. Then Hajj season — some neighbors leave for Hajj and send you letters. Eid al-Adha brings the qurbani, which ties everything you learned about zabiha together. The last restoration finishes. **Ending:** Fajr on the final day, the prayer hall is full, the garden is green, the cat is asleep on the new carpet — the masjid is alive again.

### The Ending
There is no fail state — it is a cozy game. The ending triggers when every restoration project is complete (or at the end of the Hijri year). You get a closing scene and a summary of what you built: how many rooms restored, how many hearts earned, how many iftars cooked, how many duas learned.

## 3. The World

- **The masjid** — prayer hall, wudu area, library, minaret, courtyard, garden. Every restoration visibly changes it.
- **The community kitchen** — where you cook for iftars and gatherings.
- **The market** — buy seeds and honest goods, sell your harvest.
- **The shaykh's house** — counsel, rulings, duas. The neighborhood's guide, not a boss.
- **The hunting grounds** — woods and fields outside the neighborhood.
- **The neighborhood** — houses, paths, the neighbors' gardens.

## 4. Characters

- **Imam Yusuf** — the imam. Gives the main restoration quests and teaches duas. Calm, patient, always in the masjid before Fajr.
- **Shaykh Abdullah** — the neighborhood shaykh (the "mayor" figure). The counsel system lives with him: advice, religious judgments, dhikr and duaa. See section 7.
- **Umm Maryam** — the teacher. Runs the library and the children's classes. Library restoration quests, and she asks you to grow herbs for the children's lessons.
- **Abu Khalid** — the neighbor and gardener. Teaches you farming, sells seeds at the market, and knows everything about the soil.
- **Hassan** — the cook. Runs the community kitchen, teaches the cooking minigame, and is always looking for fresh produce and halal meat.
- **Layla** — a kid from the neighborhood. Wants flowers in the garden and is the first to befriend the masjid cat. Her quests are small and sweet.
- **The masjid cat** — every masjid has one. Feed it daily and it follows you around the courtyard, sits in the garden, and sleeps on the new carpet when it is done.

## 5. The Day — The Five Prayers

Fajr to Isha sets the rhythm of the day. Work happens between prayers, and the community gathers at prayer times. Praying on time gives barakah — a small blessing that makes the day go better (energy lasts longer, crops grow a little faster). The day is divided into prayer-to-prayer blocks, so the player plans around the prayer schedule instead of a clock.

## 6. The Year — Hijri Seasons and Events

The year runs on Hijri months, and crops change with the season.

- **Muharram** — the new year, a quiet fresh start.
- **Rabi al-Awwal** — the Mawlid gathering at the masjid.
- **Rajab / Sha'ban** — preparation for Ramadan, extra community work.
- **Ramadan** — fasting month. Work shifts to the night: iftar cooking every evening, Taraweeh, Laylat al-Qadr. The biggest community moments of the game.
- **Shawwal** — Eid al-Fitr celebration in the courtyard.
- **Dhul-Hijjah** — Hajj season, neighbors travel and send letters, Eid al-Adha and the qurbani.

## 7. Core Systems

### Farming — the masjid garden
Till plots, plant, water, harvest. Produce feeds the community kitchen and sells at the market. **No chance:** yields are fixed per crop and care — no random crop quality, no lucky drops. A well-watered crop always gives its full yield.

### Restoration projects — the bundles
Each room needs specific materials and donations: the prayer hall carpet, the minaret, the wudu area, the library shelves, the garden wall, the dome. Donate materials and coins, and the room visibly changes the moment it is finished — new carpet appears, the minaret is repaired, the shelves fill with books.

### Trust — Stardew hearts
Help community members, give them produce and cooked food, finish their quests — their trust grows, Stardew-hearts style. Higher trust unlocks new dialogue, new quests, new recipes, and new knowledge. Max trust with everyone is an endgame goal.

### Cooking minigame
Cooking for the community kitchen is a timing-based minigame — stir at the right moment, flip at the right time. Skill, not luck. Good cooking feeds more people and earns more trust.

### Tile restoration minigame
Restoring the masjid's tiles is a calm pattern-and-timing minigame — place the tiles in the right order. Also skill, not luck.

### Hunting and zabiha
You can hunt in the woods — rabbits, deer, birds. Hunting itself is a simple skill minigame (sneak and aim, timing-based). **But if you want to kill an animal to cook it, you must do zabiha — the correct slaughter.** The shaykh teaches you the method first, and you cannot hunt for food until you have learned it from him. The zabiha itself is a calm, respectful step-by-step sequence: say Bismillah, face the qibla, a swift cut with a sharp blade, let the blood drain. Done correctly, the meat is halal and goes to the kitchen. Skipped or done wrong, the meat cannot be used. The same rules carry into the Eid al-Adha qurbani.

### The shaykh — counsel system
Visit the shaykh's house between prayers. Three choices:
1. **Ask for advice** — guidance on a quest or a community problem (for example, how to settle the neighbors' argument over the garden, or how to organize the iftars). Good advice unlocks the next step of a quest.
2. **Ask for a religious judgment (fatwa)** — for example, is this trade allowed? How do I do zabiha correctly? The answer comes from authentic sources and unlocks the correct way to do things in the game.
3. **Learn a dhikr or duaa** — the shaykh teaches a dhikr or duaa from authentic sources, which goes into your book of duas. Reciting it gives a small blessing for the day.

Visiting costs one time block, so the player chooses when counsel matters most. The shaykh is the neighborhood's guide — the "mayor" figure, but one you go to for guidance, not orders.

### The masjid cat
Feed it daily and it follows you around the courtyard. At full affection it sits with you while you work and sleeps on the finished carpet. Pure cozy, no mechanics pressure.

### The halal economy
Coins come from selling produce at the market and from donations as trust grows. No gambling, no loans, no interest. You can also give sadaqah — donate coins or produce — which raises community trust and barakah. A shady trader offers you a dishonest deal once in the story, and turning it down is the right choice.

### Avatars
Character creator at the start, and every option is modest:
- **Women:** hijab and abaya, in different colors and styles, plus skin tones.
- **Men:** kufi and thobe, in different colors and styles, plus skin tones.
All NPCs follow the same rule — every woman in the game wears hijab and abaya, every man wears kufi and thobe.

## 8. Art Direction

Faceless pixel figures — no faces on anyone, including the player. That is the whole art direction: simple, warm, respectful pixel art, with the masjid and garden in soft greens and golds. The masjid itself is the visual centerpiece, and it visibly comes back to life as you restore it — bare walls become tiled, the garden goes from weeds to green, the minaret is repaired.

## 9. Rules That Shape the Design

- **No faces** — faceless pixel figures for everyone.
- **No chance** — no random crop quality, no lucky drops, no loot boxes, no gacha. Fixed yields per crop and care, and every minigame is skill.
- **Halal economy only** — donations and honest trade, no gambling, no loans, no interest.
- **Authentic sources only** — any dua or knowledge the imam or shaykh teaches comes from your Drive / AICP sources, never invented.

## 10. Build Plan

**Slice 1 — the playable test (in the chat artifact first):** one courtyard, 6 garden plots, 3 crops, a Fajr-to-Isha day, water and harvest for coins, **one** restoration purchase (the prayer hall carpet), two NPCs with one task each, and the masjid cat. This tests the whole loop — work, earn, restore — in one sitting.

**Prototype — the bigger slice:** Hijri seasons with a Ramadan week, trust levels with the community members, the kitchen cooking minigame, the shaykh's counsel (advice, fatwa, dhikr), hunting with zabiha, and three restoration projects instead of one.

**Full game:** everything in this outline — the full Hijri year, all five acts, all restoration projects, all characters and quest lines, the qurbani, and the ending.

## 11. Tech Note

Stardew itself is closed source, so nothing is copied — this is inspired-by, the same way the Trail to Madinah was adapted from an MIT-licensed base. A clean starting point is Cozy Farm Starter, a tiny farming game and template in plain JavaScript with one HTML file and a config for the map, crops, prices, and texts, code under the MIT license. Verify that license in its own repo before touching it — and if it does not check out, build the slice from scratch instead.
